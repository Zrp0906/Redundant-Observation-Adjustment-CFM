# Redundant-Observation-Adjustment-CFM

## Flow Matching as a basis for multipath adjustment
The central idea of the proposed framework is that different condition–noise combinations can provide multiple estimates of the same target, and the role of Flow Matching is intrinsically tied to the construction mechanism of these candidate estimates. Unlike step-wise denoising processes, Flow Matching describes generation as a continuous transport from a simple prior distribution to the target conditional distribution through a learned vector field (Lipman et al., 2022, Liu et al., 2022). During inference, changing the combinations of initial noise and conditional information produce different transport paths and candidate realizations. 
This property is useful for the present problem because the candidate realizations are not expected to have identical prediction errors. The four conditional configurations examined in this study already show clear differences in performance, with the removal of directional information causing a particularly large degradation. Different initial noise states introduce an additional source of variation within each conditional configuration. The resulting condition–noise combinations therefore provide a set of alternative estimates of the same target with different path-specific errors.
The advantage of Flow Matching in this context is not that it is assumed to outperform diffusion models in all generation tasks. In diffusion models, the generation process relies on iterative stochastic denoising and the curved transport trajectories generally require finer temporal discretization to reduce numerical integration errors (Ho et al., 2020). Instead, the continuous and near-linear transport formulation of Flow Matching provides an explicit trajectory connecting each condition–noise pair to its generated latent state. This makes it convenient to regard each path as an identifiable candidate estimate and to perform path-level integration after generation. This relatively simple transport structure of Flow Matching therefore offers a more suitable basis for constructing redundant-observation-based adjustment strategies.
The use of Flow Matching should therefore be understood as a methodological choice that facilitates multipath estimation. The probabilistic generation process supplies alternative candidate solutions, whereas the redundant-observation adjustment determines how these candidates contribute to the final realization. The two stages address different aspects of the problem and should not be conflated.
## Relationship between redundant observations and multistep inference
Both redundant observations and multistep inference contribute to improving the performance of probabilistic spectral generation, but they influence the generation process from different perspectives. Redundant observations allow random deviations caused by noise initialization, condition perturbation, and model approximation to be compensated during fusion, thereby reducing prediction error and improving generation stability. Multiple condition-noise combinations provide different representations of the same target conditional distribution, and their weighted integration reduces the influence of anomalous realizations while maintaining the diversity required for probabilistic prediction. This mechanism is analogous to redundant measurements in classical estimation problems, where multiple imperfect observations can provide a more reliable estimate than a single measurement. In contrast, when only one trajectory is used, any deviation introduced during transport directly affects the final prediction. By integrating multiple generation paths through weighted adjustment, the proposed framework reduces the influence of individual unreliable realizations. Therefore, the benefit of redundant observations does not simply come from increasing the number of generated samples, but from exploiting the complementary information among multiple generation processes and improving the robustness of probabilistic generation. From the perspective of ocean wave modeling, this property is valuable because wave spectra are inherently stochastic and influenced by multiple interacting factors, including wind forcing, wave propagation, and historical sea states. A generation framework that only provides a single deterministic estimate may underestimate this uncertainty, whereas a probabilistic framework combined with trajectory adjustment can achieve a better balance between prediction stability and distribution representation. 
The effectiveness of redundant observations is also closely related to the quality of individual generation paths. When the conditional information is sufficient and the learned transport field accurately describes the target distribution, different trajectories tend to provide consistent estimates, and the benefit of additional observations becomes relatively limited. In contrast, when the available conditions provide incomplete constraints, redundant observations can compensate for insufficient information by introducing complementary realizations. Therefore, the improvement obtained from multipath fusion depends not only on the number of trajectories but also on the reliability and diversity of the generated paths.
Multistep inference, in contrast, mainly improves the numerical approximation of a single transport trajectory by providing more intermediate states during ODE integration. In Flow Matching, the generation process can be interpreted as a continuous transformation from noise space to data space. Increasing the number of inference steps provides more intermediate states along this transport process, allowing the numerical solution to better approximate the learned trajectory and capture complex structures in the target distribution. Therefore, improving the ODE integration of one path and combining several paths are complementary rather than competing strategies.
The experiments show that increasing the inference steps from one to five provides only a limited improvement, while increasing them further to ten produces little additional gain. Multipath fusion, in comparison, produces consistent error reductions for all three inference settings. This distinction is practically relevant. Once the learned transport field can be sufficiently approximated with a small number of integration steps, further increasing the step number may provide limited benefit to reduce discretization error. Under such circumstances, generating and fusing alternative condition–noise paths can be a more effective way to improve the final estimate than continuing to refine a single trajectory.
## Influence of latent representation and data transformation
Experimental results also demonstrate that the representation of the target spectrum has a substantial influence on generation quality. The original ocean wave energy density spectra typically exhibit strong skewness and large dynamic ranges, where energy is concentrated within limited frequency–direction regions while most spectral components remain close to zero. Such characteristics introduce difficulties for neural networks because high-energy regions may dominate optimization, while weak but physically meaningful structures receive insufficient attention (Song et al., 2025).
The logarithmic transformation followed by Z-score normalization adopted in this study alleviate this imbalance by compressing extreme values and improving the consistency of spectral distributions. In the controlled comparison, RMSE decreases from 12.78 to 7.45 and CC increases from 0.86 to 0.95. In contrast, noise reparameterization alone produces only a limited improvement, and combining it with the transformed target does not provide an additional gain. Furthermore, the CAE-based latent representation provides an additional level of distribution simplification by mapping the original spectral field into a compact feature space. Instead of directly learning the complex distribution of the full frequency–direction spectrum, LRO-CFM only needs to model the evolution of a lower-dimensional latent distribution with smoother statistical characteristics.
However, the latent-space formulation also introduces a trade-off between dimensionality reduction and spectral detail preservation. Although the dominant spectral structures are retained, localized variations around energetic spectral peaks remain more difficult to reconstruct. This limitation is consistent with the experiment that reconstruction errors are mainly associated with several high-energy regions rather than systematic deviations across the entire spectrum. Future improvements may therefore focus on adaptive latent representations or physics-guided reconstruction strategies to better preserve important spectral details.
## Balance between spectral accuracy and computational efficiency 
The comparison with other neural architectures illustrates that LRO-CFM should not be interpreted as uniformly superior in every performance measure. LRO-CFM obtains the lowest RMSE (7.10) and the highest CC (0.96), while Conv + ViT achieves lower MAE and CRPS. The computational cost, however, differs substantially: the MACs of LRO-CFM are only 0.17 G, compared with 0.93 G for U-Net + Attention and 1.58 G for Conv + ViT. 
This result suggests that the main practical advantage of LRO-CFM is the combination of competitive reconstruction accuracy and low computational cost. Performing generation in the latent space avoids applying a large generative network directly to the full-resolution spectral field, while the redundant-path adjustment provides an additional mechanism for improving the final estimate.
For applications requiring repeated spectrum estimation, the computational efficiency of the latent-space formulation may be particularly useful. The model can generate several candidate spectra and then fuse them without requiring a corresponding increase in the dimensionality of the underlying generative network.
## Limitations and future perspectives
Although the proposed framework demonstrates the capability of wave spectrum generation, several limitations remain. First, LRO-CFM is still primarily a data-driven model, and the physical constraints governing wave evolution are not explicitly embedded into the generation process. Although the generated spectra maintain important statistical and integral physical characteristics, incorporating constraints derived from wave action balance or energy conservation may further improve physical consistency, particularly under extreme and rapidly evolving sea states.
Second, the present framework relies on predefined environmental variables, including wind forcing and historical wave parameters, as conditional information. Future studies could investigate adaptive conditioning mechanisms that allow the model to incorporate heterogeneous observations with variable availability, such as remote sensing, buoy measurements, and numerical model outputs.
Finally, the current multipath adjustment strategy interprets that generated trajectories can be treated as statistically related observations of the same target distribution. Correlations among path errors influence the effectiveness of fusion. A more complete treatment of path dependence, including empirical covariance estimation and adaptive generalized least-squares adjustment, is therefore an important direction for future work.
